## Abstract

Deployment of Face Recognition systems on the edge has seen significant growth due to advancements in hardware design and efficient neural architectures. However, tailoring state-of-the-art (SOTA) Face Recognition solutions to a specific edge device remains challenging and vastly unexplored. Although benchmark data is available for some combinations of model, device, and framework, it is neither comprehensive nor scalable. 

We propose an analytical framework to characterize the relationship between a model's architectural operators and its inference efficiency across edge targets, enabling practitioners to predict latency, memory footprint, and energy consumption without exhaustive physical deployment.

---

## Latency Characterization & Edge Optimization

To model the runtime performance across heterogeneous compute blocks (CPU, GPU, Edge TPU), we characterize inference time $T(M, H)$ of a model $M$ on target hardware $H$ as:

$$
T(M, H) = \sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}(M)} \tau(l, H) + \Delta_{\text{mem}}(M, H)
$$

where $\tau(l, H)$ represents operator-level compute latency for layer $l$, and $\Delta_{\text{mem}}(M, H)$ models memory bandwidth bottleneck and tensor re-layout penalties during runtime execution.

By analyzing kernel execution bounds on embedded architectures (e.g., ARM Cortex-A72, NVIDIA Maxwell, Edge TPU), we evaluate models across diverse quantization regimes:

$$
\text{MACs}_{\text{effective}} = \alpha \cdot \text{MACs}_{\text{INT8}} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \text{MACs}_{\text{FP16}}
$$
